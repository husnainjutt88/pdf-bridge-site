ID card front and back on one page

For bank KYC, rental, job and visa applications: both sides of a card on one page, at real size, without a photocopier.

Updated 26 November 2026

Banks, landlords, employers and government offices often ask for a copy of an ID card with the front and back on one page. Photocopiers have an "ID copy" mode for this. Without one, you can make the same page from two photos.

What a good ID copy looks like

  • Both sides on one page, one above the other, on A4 (most countries) or Letter (US and Canada).
  • Real size: standard bank-card-size IDs, such as most national ID cards and driving licences, are 85.6 × 54 mm. Printed at real size, the copy matches the card.
  • Every detail readable: no glare over the photo, numbers or expiry date, nothing cut off at the edges.

Taking the photos

  1. Put the card on a dark, plain surface so its edges stand out.
  2. Hold the phone straight above the card, not at an angle, in even light. Tilt slightly if there is glare from the laminate.
  3. Take one photo of the front and one of the back.

Making the page

  1. Open the ID card copy tool on a computer and add the front and back photos.
  2. The card is found in each photo, cut out and straightened, then both sides are placed on one page at real size.
  3. Download the PDF and print it at 100% (turn off "fit to page"), or send it as it is.

Notes for specific uses

  • Pakistan (CNIC): forms often ask for both sides of the CNIC on one page; the same steps apply.
  • South Africa (Smart ID, FICA): some banks and offices ask for a certified copy, stamped and signed by a commissioner of oaths. Print the page first and take it with the original card to be certified.
  • Passports: usually only the photo page is needed; use a single photo.

Only send ID copies to organisations you trust, through their official channels. Some people add a line like "Copy for [purpose] only, [date]" across the copy, as long as it doesn't hide any details.
