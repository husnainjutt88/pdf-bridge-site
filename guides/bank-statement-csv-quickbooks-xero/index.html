Bank statement CSV for QuickBooks and Xero

The column layouts that import cleanly, and how to get there from a PDF statement.

Updated 26 November 2026

Bank feeds don't always reach back far enough, and some accounts never connect. Then you import the statement as a file. Both QuickBooks Online and Xero accept CSV, but only in particular layouts.

QuickBooks Online

QuickBooks Online imports a CSV with either three or four columns:

LayoutColumns
3 columnsDate, Description, Amount (money out as negative numbers)
4 columnsDate, Description, Credit, Debit (two separate amount columns)

Use one date format throughout, keep a single header row, and remove the opening and closing balance rows and any page totals. Intuit explains the steps in its help article on converting PDF statements. QuickBooks Desktop is different: it imports bank files in its own formats rather than plain CSV.

Xero

Xero's statement import needs at least a Date and an Amount column (money in positive, money out negative). Payee, Description and Reference columns are optional but make reconciling easier. Xero offers a CSV template on its import screen; copying your rows into it is the safest way.

From a PDF statement to an import file

  1. Convert the statement to Excel with the bank statement converter and check that the running balances match.
  2. Delete everything that isn't a transaction: balance brought forward, totals, headers repeated on each page.
  3. Arrange the columns for your software (above). For a single Amount column, use a formula like =Credit-Debit.
  4. Save as CSV UTF-8 (File › Save As).

Why imports fail

  • Dates in two formats, or day and month swapped. Format the whole column the same way.
  • Amounts stored as text, with currency symbols or thousands separators. Make them plain numbers.
  • Blank rows or extra header rows in the middle of the file.
  • Overlapping dates with transactions already imported, which creates duplicates.
