How to fill a PDF form that won't let you type

Flat and scanned forms have no boxes to click. Here is how to fill them in anyway, without printing.

Updated 26 November 2026

A PDF form can be made in two ways. A fillable form has real fields: click and type. A flat form is just a picture of a form, with printed lines and boxes, often a scan of a paper form or a document exported without fields. Clicking does nothing, so people print it, write on it and scan it back.

How to tell which kind you have

  • Open it in a PDF reader. Fillable forms highlight their fields or show a "fill in form" bar.
  • If you can't select any text at all, it's a scan: a picture of a page.

Ways to fill a flat form without printing

  1. A form filler that finds the blanks: it looks for lines, boxes and tick boxes on the page, reads the label next to each one, and gives you a list to type into. Your answers are written onto the right places in the PDF. This is what the PDF form filler does, including for scanned forms.
  2. Add text boxes by hand in a PDF editor: draw a text box over each line and type. It works but takes time on long forms, and text can drift off the lines.

Tips for a clean result

  • For forms with one letter per box (common on government forms), type the answer in capitals; each character is placed in its own box.
  • Use the date format the form shows, for example DD/MM/YYYY.
  • Check every page before sending: open the filled PDF and zoom in.
  • If the form needs a handwritten signature, print the filled form, sign it, and scan it back with photos to PDF. Signing inside the PDF is in the PDF Bridge app, coming soon.
