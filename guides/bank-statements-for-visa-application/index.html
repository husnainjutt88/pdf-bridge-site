Bank statements for a visa application

What visa officers look at, and how to turn a pile of monthly PDFs into a clean, complete file.

Updated 26 November 2026

Most visa applications ask for proof that you can pay for your trip or stay, and bank statements are the usual proof. Requirements differ by country and visa type, so always follow the checklist for your visa. The steps below fit most of them.

What is usually checked

  • Your name and account number on every page, matching your passport.
  • The period covered: checklists often ask for the most recent 3 to 6 months, with no missing pages or months.
  • Regular income such as salary credits, and the balance over time, not only on the last day. A large deposit just before applying often needs an explanation.

Official or downloaded statements?

Some checklists accept statements downloaded from online banking; others ask for statements stamped and signed by the bank, or a separate bank letter. If yours asks for stamped statements, request them from your branch in good time. If downloaded PDFs are accepted, download each month's official statement rather than taking screenshots of the app.

Preparing the file

  1. Download every month in the period, all pages of each.
  2. Open locked PDFs: many banks email statements protected with a password, often based on your date of birth or ID number. Upload portals may refuse locked files, so save an unlocked copy if your PDF reader allows it.
  3. Put the months in order and combine them into one PDF if the portal takes one file per document type.
  4. Check the size limit. Upload portals often cap each file. See how to make a PDF small enough for an online form.
  5. Translations: if your statements aren't in the language the embassy asks for, check whether it needs a certified translation.

Check your own numbers first

Before you submit, it helps to see what the officer will see: total money in each month, your lowest balance, and any unusual deposits. Converting the statements to Excel makes this quick: sort by amount, add up the credits per month, and look at the balance column.

Applying from Pakistan or India? Your CNIC or ID copy and the statements are often uploaded together, and both need to be clear and readable after compressing.
