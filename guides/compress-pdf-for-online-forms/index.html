How to make a PDF small enough for an online form

Visa, job and exam portals often cap uploads at 100 KB, 200 KB or 1 MB. Here is how to get under the limit and keep the text readable.

Updated 26 November 2026

A PDF made from a phone photo or a scanner is mostly a large picture of each page. That picture is what makes it big, so shrinking a scanned PDF means making those pictures smaller without making the text unreadable.

Why your scan is so large

  • High resolution: a phone photo can be 12 megapixels or more, far more than a page needs.
  • Colour: a colour scan of black text on white paper stores three times the information of a grayscale one.
  • Each page is a separate picture, so a 5-page document is five photos.

Ways to get under the limit

  1. Compress the PDF. Use a compressor first: it reduces the pictures' resolution and quality. "Balanced" settings usually cut a scanned document a lot while keeping text sharp.
  2. Fit an exact limit. If a portal says "maximum 100 KB", a tool that targets a size tries progressively smaller settings until the file fits, and keeps the best quality that does.
  3. Scan in grayscale if colour isn't required. Text documents lose nothing.
  4. Send only the pages needed. Split off pages the form doesn't ask for.
  5. Crop the edges. Table or carpet around the page is wasted space.

Rough guide to what fits

LimitWhat usually fits
50–100 KBOne page of text in grayscale, reduced resolution. Small print gets soft.
200–300 KBOne or two pages comfortably, often in colour.
1 MBSeveral pages with clear text and colour.

These are rough figures; pages with photos, stamps or dense text need more room.

Always open the compressed file and zoom in on the smallest text before you upload it. If it's hard to read, use the next size up and drop pages or colour instead.
