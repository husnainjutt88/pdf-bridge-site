How to convert a bank statement PDF to Excel

Three ways that work, which one fits your statement, and how to check the spreadsheet before you rely on it.

Updated 26 November 2026

A bank statement PDF looks like a table, but inside the file it is just text placed at positions on a page. That is why copying and pasting it into Excel usually goes wrong. There are three reliable ways to get the transactions into a spreadsheet. Which one is best depends on where the statement came from.

Which method fits your statement?

Your situationBest method
A recent statement for an account you can log in to1. Download CSV or Excel from online banking
A digital PDF (you can select the text) and Excel for Microsoft 365 on Windows2. Excel's own PDF import
An older statement, a PDF someone emailed you, a scan or a photo, or a password-protected PDF3. A bank statement converter

1. Download a CSV from online banking

Most banks let you download recent transactions as CSV or Excel from the account's transaction list, usually with a button called Export, Download or Spreadsheet. A CSV opens straight in Excel, Google Sheets or Numbers.

The limits: the download usually covers only a recent window (often a few months to a couple of years), it only works for accounts you can log in to, and the columns can differ from the statement itself. Official statements are often PDF only. See getting older or PDF-only statements into Excel.

2. Use Excel's PDF import (Windows)

Excel for Microsoft 365 on Windows can read tables from a PDF: Data › Get Data › From File › From PDF, pick the file, then choose the table for each page in the Navigator. It works well on simple digital statements. It does not read scanned or photographed statements (there is no text in them to read), each page often arrives as a separate table you have to combine, and transactions whose description runs over two lines can split into extra rows.

3. Use a bank statement converter

A converter built for statements reads the whole document as one table, keeps each transaction on one row, and turns amounts and dates into real Excel values. This is the way to go for scans, photos, statements from closed accounts, and statements that clients or tenants send you as PDFs.

  1. Open the bank statement to Excel converter on a computer.
  2. Drop in the statement PDF. If it is locked, type its password (often your date of birth or part of your account number); it is used only to open that file.
  3. Download the .xlsx file and open it in Excel or Google Sheets.

Check the spreadsheet before you use it

A converted statement is only useful if every row is right. Three quick checks catch almost every problem:

  • Running balance: for each row, the previous balance plus credits minus debits should equal the balance on that row. PDF Bridge does this for you and lists any row that doesn't match on a separate Checks sheet.
  • Totals: add up the money in and money out columns and compare them with the totals printed on the statement, if it has them.
  • Numbers, not text: click an amount. If Excel shows it left-aligned or warns "Number stored as text", sums will be wrong. See fixing a statement that lands in one column.

Need the statement for accounting software rather than Excel? QuickBooks and Xero want specific CSV columns. See bank statement CSV for QuickBooks and Xero.
