Bank statements for a home loan, mortgage or rental

What lenders, letting agents and landlords look for, and how to check your statements before you send them.

Updated 26 November 2026

Lenders and landlords ask for bank statements to see that your income is real and regular and that you can afford the payment. The exact number of months varies by lender, so use their checklist. These are the usual patterns.

By country

WhereUsually asked for
South Africa (home loans, vehicle finance, rentals)Recent statements, commonly the last 3 months, with payslips. Bond originators and rental agents often accept the PDFs your bank emails or lets you download.
United Kingdom (mortgages, renting)Recent months of statements for income and spending checks; letting agents and referencing companies may ask for proof of income such as statements or payslips.
United States (mortgages, apartments)Mortgage lenders often ask for the most recent two or more monthly statements for each account, all pages; large deposits may need a letter explaining them. Landlords may ask for statements or pay stubs.

What they look for

  • Regular income matching your payslips.
  • Existing commitments: other loan repayments, debit orders, subscriptions.
  • How the account is run: returned debit orders, unpaid items or a permanent overdraft count against you.
  • Complete documents: every page, your name and account number visible, no edits.

Check your statements first

  1. Convert each month to Excel with the bank statement converter; every running balance is checked, so you know nothing was misread.
  2. Total your income and your fixed costs per month. This is roughly the affordability picture the lender will see.
  3. Look for anything that needs explaining, such as a large one-off deposit or a returned payment, and prepare a short note.

Send the original PDFs to the lender or agent, not the spreadsheet. The spreadsheet is for checking your own numbers.
