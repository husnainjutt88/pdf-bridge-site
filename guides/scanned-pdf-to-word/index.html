Scanned PDF to editable Word

Why a scanned PDF comes out as a picture in Word, and how to get text you can actually edit.

Updated 26 November 2026

Convert a scanned PDF to Word with a basic converter and you often get a Word file containing one big picture per page. Nothing to edit. That's because a scan has no text in it, only an image of text.

Digital PDF or scan?

Try to select a word in the PDF. If you can, it's a digital PDF and converts directly. If the whole page selects as one block, or nothing selects at all, it's a scan and needs OCR (optical character recognition) to turn the picture of letters into real text.

What OCR needs to work well

  • A sharp, straight page: good light, no shadow across the text, the page flat. Tilted pages are straightened automatically by good OCR, but blur can't be fixed.
  • Enough resolution: a phone photo of a full page is usually plenty; tiny print needs a closer photo.
  • The right language: OCR reads one writing system far better when it knows which one to expect, especially for Arabic, Urdu, Hindi or Chinese.

Keeping the layout

Plain OCR gives you a stream of text. To get a Word document that looks like the page, the converter also has to recognise columns, tables and headings and rebuild them in Word. Check tables after converting: numbers in narrow columns are where OCR most often slips.

Steps

  1. Open PDF to Word (or image to text for photos and screenshots) on a computer.
  2. Add the scanned PDF or photos.
  3. Download the Word document, then read through it once, paying attention to names, numbers and dates.
