Bank statement pastes into one column in Excel

Why copying a statement out of a PDF goes wrong, and how to fix each of the usual problems.

Updated 26 November 2026

A PDF doesn't store a table. It stores pieces of text and where to draw them. When you copy from a PDF, you get those pieces in reading order, with spaces or line breaks between them, and Excel has no idea where one column ends and the next begins. Here are the usual symptoms and what to do about each.

Everything lands in column A

Each line of the statement arrives as one cell. You can split it with Data › Text to Columns (Delimited, with Space as the delimiter), but descriptions contain spaces too, so a payee like "Northwind Trading Ltd" ends up across three columns and the amounts move with it. It works for very simple statements; for most it is quicker to convert the PDF properly.

One transaction becomes two or three rows

When a description wraps onto a second line in the PDF, the second line arrives as its own row with no date or amount. You have to find each one and join it back. A statement converter keeps the wrapped text with its transaction, which is the main reason to use one.

Amounts are text, so SUM gives 0

Pasted amounts often keep thousands separators, currency symbols, a trailing minus or "CR"/"DR" labels, so Excel treats them as text. Signs of the problem: amounts are left-aligned, a small green triangle appears, or SUM returns 0.

  • Remove currency symbols and "CR"/"DR" with Find and Replace.
  • Select the column and use the warning menu's Convert to Number, or Text to Columns › Finish.
  • If your statement uses 1.234,56 style numbers and Excel uses 1,234.56 (or the other way round), set the separators in Text to Columns › Advanced.

Dates come out wrong

A date like 03/04/2026 can mean 3 April or 4 March. If the statement's date order differs from your computer's, Excel either swaps day and month or leaves the date as text. In Text to Columns, set that column to Date and pick the order the statement uses (DMY or MDY).

Merged cells from a converter

Some general PDF converters copy the page's look, so you get merged cells and empty spacer columns. Unmerge everything (Home › Merge & Center › Unmerge Cells), then delete the empty columns. A converter made for statements gives you one plain row per transaction instead.

Quick check after any fix: work out each row's balance from the one before it. If every balance matches the statement, nothing was lost.
